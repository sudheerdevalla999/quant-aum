A furious king points at a stone pillar and asks his young son one question: *Is your God in this too?*

The king is Hiranyakashipu. The boy is Prahlada. In the Bhagavata Purana, Prahlada does not hesitate. His Lord, he says, is in the pillar, in the sword in the king's hand, in the king himself. In everything.

The king strikes the pillar. It splits open, and out comes Narasimha: neither man nor lion, at neither day nor night, on neither the inside nor the outside of the palace.

For most of us this is a childhood story. But Prahlada's answer is also a very precise claim about matter. *The divine is not somewhere else. It is inside the stone.* Modern physics has spent a century looking inside the stone. What it found makes Prahlada's answer worth hearing again.

## What a pillar is really made of

Zoom into the stone and it stops looking solid. It is made of atoms, so small that a single grain of sand holds tens of quintillions of them.

Each atom has a tiny, heavy core called the nucleus. Inside it sit **protons**, which carry a positive charge, and **neutrons**, which carry none. Around the nucleus move **electrons**, light and negatively charged.

Here is the surprise. An atom is almost entirely empty space. If the nucleus were a marble placed in the middle of a cricket ground, the electrons would be specks out near the boundary rope. What holds it all together is not stuff but **force**: invisible pulls and pushes that keep every particle in its place.

So a pillar is not really a lump of matter. It is three kinds of particle, held in a pattern by energy. Four things, in every piece of the world. And the Indian tradition also speaks of four: Brahma, Vishnu, Shiva and Shakti.

## Four principles inside every atom

The Trimurti are three functions of one reality: creation, preservation and dissolution. Shakti is the power that drives all three. Set them beside the atom and the fit is striking.

### Brahma and the proton

Brahma gives things their form. In the atom, that job belongs to the proton. The number of protons decides what an atom *is*. One proton makes hydrogen. Six make carbon. Seventy-nine make gold. Change that number and you have created a different element.

### Vishnu and the neutron

Vishnu preserves order. In the nucleus, neutrons do something similar. Protons all carry the same charge, so they push each other away. Neutrons add the strong nuclear "glue" without adding any push, and that keeps the nucleus stable.

### Shiva and the electron

Shiva dissolves and transforms. In the atom, change happens at the electrons. Every chemical reaction, from a burning lamp to the digestion of your food, is electrons leaving, joining or being shared. Old bonds break, new ones form.

### Shakti and the forces

Shakti is the energy behind all action. The electromagnetic force holds electrons to the nucleus and holds atoms to each other. Inside the nucleus, the strong force does the work. Like the Devi, who appears as Durga, Lakshmi, Saraswati and Kali, Shakti here takes many forms.

## Going deeper: the dance, not the dancers

Those four matches are a good way in, but the tradition never treated the Trimurti as three objects. They are three *activities*. And physics finds all three going on inside matter, all the time.

- **Creation (*srishti*).** Concentrated energy can turn into a pair of brand-new particles. Inside stars, hydrogen fuses into helium, carbon and oxygen. Almost every atom in your body was built this way, in a star that lived and died before the Sun was born.
- **Preservation (*sthiti*).** Conservation laws keep the total amount of energy and charge constant through every change. I wrote about this in [Sati, Parvati and the Conservation of Energy]({% post_url 2026-11-07-sati-parvati-conservation-of-energy %}). Binding forces hold patterns together for billions of years.
- **Transformation (*samhara*).** In radioactive decay, the weak force turns a neutron into a proton. Nothing is destroyed. One form gives way to the next, which is exactly how Shaiva tradition describes Shiva's work.

Now look at Shakti again. Einstein's E = mc² says that mass and energy are one thing in two states. But there is something even more surprising. Most of a proton's mass does not come from the tiny particles inside it. Roughly 99% of it comes from the *energy* binding those particles together.

In other words, the solid stone is mostly frozen energy. The *Saundarya Lahari* says Shiva without Shakti cannot even stir. Physics agrees in its own way: take the energy out of matter and almost nothing is left.

<div><b>Tradition</b>Shiva without Shakti is <em>shava</em>, a lifeless body. Form without power is nothing.</div>

There is one more layer. As I described in [Quantum and Aum]({% post_url 2026-11-07-quantum-and-aum %}) and [Shiva and Stillness]({% post_url 2026-11-22-shiva-and-stillness %}), every particle is a ripple in a field that fills all of space. The electron field is present in the pillar, in the air around it and in empty space alike. A particle is simply where that field is vibrating.

That is the closest physics comes to Prahlada's answer. The opening verse of the Isha Upanishad says it in four words: *īśā vāsyam idaṁ sarvam*. In my own rendering: *all of this is clothed in the Lord.*

## Same building blocks, endless forms

Three particles and a handful of forces make everything: about 118 elements, millions of molecules, every rock, river and living cell.

Carbon shows how far one ingredient can go. Arrange its atoms one way and you get the soft graphite in a pencil. Arrange them another way and you get diamond. Combine carbon with a few other elements and you get DNA, the molecule that carries life.

The tradition says the same about the divine. One reality takes form after form: the fish, the tortoise, the boar, Narasimha, Rama, Krishna. Each avatar is the same essence in a new arrangement.

And each arrives for a reason. In the Bhagavad Gita (4.7–8), Krishna says he takes form age after age to protect the good and to restore dharma.

Narasimha is the clearest case of all. Hiranyakashipu's boon said no man or beast could kill him, by day or by night, indoors or outdoors, on the ground or in the sky, with any weapon. So the form that appeared was exactly the form the moment needed. Half man and half lion. At twilight. On the threshold of the palace. Across Narasimha's own lap. With claws, not weapons. The shape followed the purpose, down to the last detail.

Chemistry shows a faint echo of this. An enzyme's shape fits the one molecule it works on, like a key in a lock. But here the bridge needs care. Physics can describe *how* matter takes a form. It does not say *why*. Purpose is where the scriptures speak and the equations go quiet.

## Where the analogy breaks

<div class="note"><strong>Keeping my promise:</strong> The particle matches above are a lens, not a lab result. The most common kind of hydrogen has no neutron at all, so if Vishnu were "the neutron", the most abundant atom in the universe would have no preserver. A neutron left on its own decays in about fifteen minutes, which is an odd trait for a preserver. Protons do not "create", and electrons do not "destroy"; positive and negative are just labels for two kinds of charge. That is why I think the deeper, truer correlation is with the <em>processes</em> inside matter (creation, preservation, transformation and the energy behind them) rather than with particular particles. And physics does not test for the divine. Nothing here proves that God is in the pillar. What physics does is remove an old obstacle to that idea: the picture of the world as dead, solid stuff.</div>

## Where science stops and Advaita begins

Adi Shankara's Advaita takes one more step. In Advaita, Brahma, Vishnu, Shiva and Shakti belong to the level of appearance. They are how the one reality, Brahman, shows up as a world. Shankara calls the power behind that appearance *maya*, the Lord's own Shakti.

That is precisely the level physics studies: forms, forces, changes. It measures the dance beautifully. But there is something it never measures, because it cannot be put under an instrument: the one who knows the dance. Vedanta calls it the *sakshi*, the witness.

So this is the real bridge. Physics shows that everything is one field in many forms. Advaita asks what that field ultimately is, and answers with three words from the Chandogya Upanishad: *tat tvam asi*. You are That.

## Back to the pillar

Hiranyakashipu saw a pillar as dead stone, and the world as something to control. Prahlada saw the same pillar alive with the Lord.

Today we can look at the stone the way a physicist does. It is creation, preservation and transformation happening at every moment. It is held together by an energy that is almost all of what it is, rippling in fields that fill all of space.

The scriptures never needed physics to be true. But physics has given us new eyes for an old answer. When someone says God is everywhere and in everything, the stone no longer argues back.

*Is He in this pillar?* Look closely enough, and the better question becomes: where is He not?
